Daniel I. Copeland, MD, MSc

Physician-Engineer | Digital Health | Mind-Body Medicine

I am a physician-engineer whose work focuses on translating physiological and behavioral data into meaningful insights about human health and performance. My research and product work sit at the intersection of sensing technology, clinical science, and digital health, with an emphasis on how objective biological signals can help us better understand stress, recovery, resilience, and mental well-being.

My work spans wearable and environmental sensing, longitudinal physiology, behavioral interventions, and AI-enabled health systems. I am particularly interested in how signals such as sleep, cardiovascular dynamics, temperature regulation, movement, and activity patterns can be used to develop scalable, low-burden tools for monitoring health in real-world settings.

A central theme of my work is designing systems that are informative and motivating without becoming intrusive. I am deeply interested in how health technologies can support awareness and behavior change while still preserving presence, autonomy, and flow. In many ways, I think about this through the analogy of wave-particle duality: modern sensing technologies allow us to observe reflections of human experience without fully collapsing that experience into discrete measurements. Rather than decomposing life entirely into data points, I am interested in studying the patterns, rhythms, and signatures that emerge while still preserving the integrity of the lived experience itself.

My broader goal is to help build technologies that make health measurement more continuous, personalized, and actionable, bridging the gap between clinical medicine, consumer health, and everyday life.
